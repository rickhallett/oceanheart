# Marketable overlap opportunity scan

Date: 25 August 2026

Status: research only. No offer has been sold, no buyer has been interviewed, and no demand claim is validated.

## Verdict

Only one hypothesis deserves an immediate sales test: a short clinician-led interaction and safety evaluation for a mental-health AI team. There is direct evidence that companies pay clinicians to test conversations, write scenarios, identify failure modes, and improve escalation logic. A recent Limbic contract offered a UK CBT psychologist or psychotherapist £50 per hour for an 80-hour evaluation sprint. Vals AI currently advertises $75 to $100 per hour for comparable mental-health AI safety work. These are hiring signals, not proof that either company would buy an external consultancy.

Synthetic testing belongs inside that service as a cheap formative method. It is not a separate product and must not be described as regulatory usability evidence. Therapy-practice governance has a real incoming professional obligation but weak spending evidence. A standalone progressive-legitimisation audit has no observable category demand and should remain an internal lens unless a buyer pays for a manual review.

| Rank | Hypothesis | Verdict | Evidence that matters |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Clinician-led AI interaction and safety evaluation | **TEST FIRST** | Recent first-party UK contract at £50/hour for almost the exact overlap; current mental-health evaluation contracting at $75 to $100/hour |
| 2 | Practitioner-led synthetic interaction testing | **USE AS A METHOD, NOT A BUSINESS** | Vendors buy clinical scenario design and red-teaming, but MHRA human-factors guidance still requires representative intended users |
| 3 | Bounded AI governance for therapy practices or vendors | **HOLD, THEN TEST SMALL** | BACP's 2026 framework creates specific duties, but its own guidance and training are strong substitutes and practitioner spend is unproven |
| 4 | Progressive-legitimisation and evidence-debt audit | **CUT AS A STANDALONE OFFER** | AI assurance spend exists, but no primary source shows a buyer seeking this problem or language |

The anti-sycophancy rule for all four is simple: an obligation is not a budget, a job advert is not a consulting client, an incumbent proves a category exists rather than that a new entrant can win, and a paid deposit is the first demand evidence.

## 1. Clinician-led AI interaction and safety evaluation

**Observable buyer.** The buyer is a clinical, product, safety, or evaluation lead at a mental-health or wellbeing company with a patient-facing conversational product. The strongest targets are teams already shipping multi-turn interactions and making decisions about when the system should respond, pause, refuse, or hand off.

**Existing spend or obligation.** A recent Limbic four-week contractor role asked an experienced CBT psychologist or psychotherapist to pressure-test clinical quality, efficacy, and safety, create prompts, and test different models. It paid £50 per hour for 20 hours per week. The application deadline was 7 June 2026, so this is recent spend evidence, not a live lead. Vals AI currently advertises $75 to $100 per hour for a mental-health clinician to review conversations, create scenarios and rubrics, and identify safety failures. RadiantGraph advertises $35 to $50 per hour for health-coach review of voice-agent transcripts, escalation signals, privacy boundaries, and conversation flows. [Limbic contract](https://jobs.ashbyhq.com/limbic/afff9754-a5fa-44d5-80c7-d968405355ee), [Vals AI contract](https://jobs.ashbyhq.com/vals-ai/eebf2b72-8996-41bb-b780-582829c2a636), [RadiantGraph contract](https://jobs.ashbyhq.com/radiantgraph/70b080cb-a70d-46bc-a701-cb8480f99e8f)

The obligation side is also real. NHS England requires suppliers of health IT used in the NHS to complete DCB0129 clinical safety work, while deploying organisations complete DCB0160 work. Current ambient-scribe guidance calls for hazard logs, safety cases, monitoring, prospective-user validation, and ongoing output audits. These duties create work, but formal clinical-safety sign-off is a different service from a psychotherapist-led interaction review. [NHS England clinical-risk review](https://www.england.nhs.uk/long-read/national-review-of-clinical-risk-management-standardsdcb0129-and-dcb0160-supporting-information/), [NHS England ambient-scribe guidance](https://www.england.nhs.uk/long-read/guidance-on-the-use-of-ai-enabled-ambient-scribing-products-in-health-and-care-settings/)

**Incumbents and substitutes.** Internal clinical product teams are the main substitute. Limbic, Neko Health, Hippocratic AI, and Vals AI all advertise dedicated clinical evaluation or safety roles. Specialist clinical-safety firms sell readiness reviews, safety cases, CSO cover, and post-market monitoring. Patient Guard lists clinical-safety projects from £6,500. This is a credential-heavy market, not empty space. [Neko Health clinical AI role](https://jobs.ashbyhq.com/neko-health/bd9705cf-e6c1-43f0-84df-c7a636ec7d98), [Hippocratic AI clinical validation role](https://jobs.ashbyhq.com/Hippocratic%20AI/d6499382-8441-4872-a996-45268fb5885b), [Patient Guard services and pricing](https://patientguard.com/services/clinical-safety-compliance-services/)

**Why Kai may be credible.** The supplied profile has a rare but legible combination: 15 years of CBT practice, direct knowledge of how risk and rupture appear over a conversation, and the ability to turn those judgments into executable evaluations, traces, deterministic boundaries, and human handoffs. That is most credible when limited to interaction quality, scope control, escalation, and product-team feedback. It does not establish medical-device, NHS procurement, or formal CSO authority.

**Missing proof.** Current professional registration and indemnity for this work need verification. There is no paid healthcare-AI evaluation case study, externally accepted rubric, inter-rater study, buyer reference, or evidence that Kai's engineering contribution improves an evaluation enough to command more than clinician-contractor rates. Limbic required BABCP registration. Several broader healthcare roles prefer a nurse, doctor, PhD, or accredited CSO.

**Fastest paid-or-declined test.** Offer one UK mental-health AI team a fixed 20-hour sprint at £1,000, matching the observed £50 per hour UK rate. The team supplies a staging product and synthetic cases. Kai returns 12 multi-turn test conversations, reproducible failures, an act/pause/escalate analysis, and a one-hour findings review. Approach no more than eight named teams with an active conversational product. Count only a paid deposit or an explicit decline. Calls, compliments, and requests to stay in touch do not count.

**Kill condition.** Stop after eight qualified offers if none pays. Also stop or narrow the offer if buyers consistently want formal clinical-safety sign-off, large-scale annotation, or academic validation that Kai cannot presently provide.

## 2. Bounded AI governance and workflow implementation for therapy practices or vendors

**Observable buyer.** The buyer is a BACP member, group-practice owner, clinical director, or BACP-accredited service that already uses an AI note tool, transcription service, chatbot, or automated administrative workflow. A practitioner with no planned AI use is not a buyer.

**Existing spend or obligation.** BACP's 2026 Ethical Framework requires members to assess an AI or digital tool before use, understand data handling, disclose benefits and risks, obtain informed consent before entering personal data, and retain responsibility for decisions. It becomes mandatory in November 2026. In BACP's 2024 member survey, 84.6% of 4,976 respondents said they did not knowingly use AI in practice and 11.6% said they did. Members reported concerns about recommendation accuracy, lack of user knowledge, privacy, and bias. This establishes an implementation problem for some practitioners. It does not establish willingness to pay. [BACP 2026 Ethical Framework](https://www.bacp.co.uk/media/26472/bacp-ethical-framework-for-the-counselling-professions-2026.pdf), [BACP AI member-survey summary](https://www.bacp.co.uk/media/23586/artificial-intelligence-and-technology-in-therapy-member-survey-report-executive-summary-18.docx)

For regulated health providers, CQC's current AI guidance expects risk assessment, human oversight, training, a DPIA, monitoring, incident routes, and compliant procurement. The ICO's AI risk toolkit likewise connects AI risks to UK GDPR and says organisations should use DPIAs where processing is likely to create high risk. [CQC AI expectations](https://www.cqc.org.uk/about-us/transparency/artificial-intelligence-health-social-care-cqcs-role-expectations-plans), [ICO AI risk toolkit](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/artificial-intelligence/guidance-on-ai-and-data-protection/ai-and-data-protection-risk-toolkit/)

**Incumbents and substitutes.** BACP is producing e-learning, text guidance, videos, and ethics resources for the new framework. Supervisors, DPOs, insurers, software vendors, and specialist clinical-safety consultancies are established substitutes. For NHS-facing work, accredited CSOs and firms with DCB0129, DCB0160, DTAC, medical-device, and data-protection experience have much stronger proof.

**Why Kai may be credible.** A practising therapist who can inspect data flow and automation boundaries can translate abstract guidance into a real practice workflow. The credible output is a decision record, data map, consent and transparency gaps, a human-responsibility map, and a short implementation plan. It is not legal advice, a DPIA sign-off, a medical-device opinion, or a clinical-safety case.

**Missing proof.** There is no evidence that independent or group practices buy this service, no price evidence, no approved template, no insurer or professional-body endorsement, and no proof that the work stays cleanly inside Kai's competence and indemnity. The 84.6% non-use figure may mean low urgency rather than latent demand.

**Fastest paid-or-declined test.** Offer a £250, two-hour AI-use review to ten named practices that confirm they already use an AI tool. The deliverable is one current-state map and a bounded action list against the BACP duties. Use synthetic examples and never inspect client data. Do not build templates or a course before a payment.

**Kill condition.** Stop after ten qualified offers if fewer than two pay. If buyers only want inexpensive general education, reduce this to a workshop or leave the category to BACP and supervisors.

## 3. Audit for progressive legitimisation and evidence debt

**Observable buyer.** The proposed buyer is a founder, head of product, innovation lead, or investor with an AI initiative that has accumulated prototypes, names, plans, and internal support without equivalent evidence of need or return.

**Existing spend or obligation.** The UK AI-assurance market is real but broad. DSIT estimates more than £1 billion in current market value, identified 161 UK assurance firms, and committed an £11 million innovation fund. A £40,000 NICE contract in 2026 covered ethical AI adoption, governance, safeguards, and prioritisation frameworks. None of this evidence names progressive legitimisation, evidence debt, premature product reality, or portfolio stop decisions as a purchased service. [DSIT third-party assurance roadmap](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/trusted-third-party-ai-assurance-roadmap/trusted-third-party-ai-assurance-roadmap), [NICE AI governance contract](https://www.find-tender.service.gov.uk/Notice/007921-2026)

**Incumbents and substitutes.** AI assurance firms, large consultancies, ISO 42001 certification bodies, product coaches, boards, investors, and ordinary discovery or stage-gate reviews cover adjacent territory. Credo AI, Holistic AI, Deloitte, EY, and BSI already sell governance, readiness, technical validation, monitoring, and certification. DSIT's own AIME consultation found that a general, sector-agnostic self-assessment struggled to meet different users' needs. [DSIT AIME response](https://www.gov.uk/government/consultations/ai-management-essentials-tool/outcome/guidance-for-using-the-ai-management-essentials-tool-government-response), [Deloitte AI assurance](https://www.deloitte.com/uk/en/services/audit-assurance/services/ai-assurance.html), [BSI ISO 42001](https://www.bsigroup.com/en-GB/products-and-services/standards/iso-42001-ai-management-system/)

**Why Kai may be credible.** The morning's frozen app ecosystem is a strong worked example of the phenomenon. Kai also understands agent workflows well enough to inspect how names, prototypes, evaluator language, and repeated assistant agreement can manufacture confidence. That gives him a sharp lens. One self-case does not establish a repeatable service.

**Missing proof.** There is no external case, agreed definition, buyer language, causal evidence, repeatable method, independent-rater reliability, or proof that a buyer will pay to hear that its project should stop. The label itself could repeat the failure by making one observation feel like a mature discipline.

**Fastest paid-or-declined test.** Offer a manual review of one existing AI initiative for £750. The buyer supplies the decision history, prototypes, claimed evidence, costs, and current go/no-go question. The output is a two-page evidence ledger and a stop, test, or continue recommendation. Make no framework, score, dashboard, or named product first. Put it to no more than eight leaders who can identify a current project that may be overbuilt.

**Kill condition.** Stop after eight qualified offers if none pays. Stop earlier if the review cannot be distinguished from generic product discovery, or if two independent reviewers cannot apply its central distinction consistently to the same record.

## 4. Practitioner-led synthetic testing of high-consequence agent interactions

**Observable buyer.** The buyer is an AI product or evaluation lead who needs realistic multi-turn clinical scenarios before involving patients, clinicians, or live data. The purchased output would be scenarios, executable tests, clinician adjudication, and traceable findings.

**Existing spend or obligation.** The same Limbic and Vals roles show direct spend on clinicians who create scenarios, test conversations, and define evaluation criteria. Dritiva offers outsourced clinical annotation, red-teaming, and patient-interaction testing. Specialist human-factors firms such as BERGO sell formative evaluation, summative studies, use-risk analysis, and regulatory documentation. [Dritiva clinical evaluation services](https://www.dritiva.com/), [BERGO human-factors services](https://www.bergo.co.uk/)

There is an important limit. MHRA guidance says formative and summative human-factors studies should use participants who represent intended users. Summative work must include representatives from every identified user group in a realistic simulated or actual environment. An LLM playing a patient is therefore a scenario generator or test driver, not a substitute for representative human participants or regulatory usability validation. [MHRA human-factors guidance](https://www.gov.uk/government/uploads/system/uploads/attachment_data/file/645862/HumanFactors_Medical-Devices_v1.0.pdf)

**Incumbents and substitutes.** Internal evaluation engineers, contracted clinicians, annotation pools, clinical red-team firms, human-factors consultancies, and real-user formative research all compete. Large scenario libraries are easy to copy. The defensible contribution would have to be judgment about conversational process, subtle risk development, scope boundaries, and handoff, plus a runnable and inspectable test harness.

**Why Kai may be credible.** The supplied background supports both halves of the work: constructing realistic CBT and distress trajectories, and implementing deterministic checks, traces, replay, and escalation boundaries. This is stronger than prompt writing alone. It remains unproved with an external buyer.

**Missing proof.** There is no validated scenario set, inter-rater calibration, comparison with real-user failures, research protocol, external acceptance, or evidence that the engineering component saves a buyer time. The phrase "synthetic usability testing" overclaims. "Clinician-led synthetic interaction pre-test" is the honest description.

**Fastest paid-or-declined test.** Offer one £1,500 pre-test of a staging conversational system: 20 synthetic multi-turn scenarios, executable replay, clinician adjudication, and five prioritised failures. Limit it to formative product feedback and state in the proposal that it is not clinical validation or human-factors evidence. Ask six qualified teams that already run conversational evals.

**Kill condition.** Do not sell it separately if buyers treat it as one part of the evaluation sprint in hypothesis 1. Stop after six qualified offers if none pays, or if buyers want commodity scenario volume, formal usability evidence, or clinical-research claims instead of the bounded pre-test.

## Decision

Run one sales test, not four. Start with hypothesis 1 and include synthetic scenarios only when useful. Do not create a service page, methodology, certification language, or additional prototype before a paid deposit. If the first eight qualified buyers decline, preserve the research and stop.

The progressive-legitimisation example should remain a governance case and internal veto: no name, interface, architecture, or research volume upgrades a hypothesis to a business. Only external behaviour does that.
