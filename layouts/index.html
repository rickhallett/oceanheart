I can hold the relationship and debug the system.

My scarce product is attention: the ability to perceive what is happening in a person and a system, respond across several registers, and design the next move.

I spent fifteen years finding the real problem with people, then learned to build and support the systems that were supposed to solve it. The work now sits between therapist, experience designer and engineer. I can stay with silence, ask the hard question, make a boundary explicit, or trace a failure through software until it has an owner and a recovery path.

Oceanheart is the name for that way of working. It is not a technique yet. It becomes one through use, in public, with the people who come through these doors.

Meet the machine; master yourself.

An Oceanheart project · Research stage

You talk with a frontier model about something that matters to you, and watch what happens in you as you do: what you expect, what you trust, where your body tightens, when you defer, and when you push back.

I am in the loop from the first exchange and I follow up with you afterwards. What we learn goes back into the design.

Your role
Bring a real question and stay curious about the encounter.
My role
Set the frame, remain in the loop, and follow up personally.
Early audience
Therapists and wellbeing practitioners first.
Status
Early research and design, with participation by invitation.

Before anything begins, you will know exactly what the model sees, what is kept, who can read it, and how to stop. Nothing about you is used for research without your specific agreement.

How I work

Whether the constraint is in a person, a relationship, an encounter with a machine, or a workflow, the sequence does not change.

  1. Enter

    Enter the real context

    The conversation, model exchange, or workflow as it actually happens.

  2. Find

    Find the live constraint

    Name what is holding, what triggers it, what it costs, and who is carrying it.

  3. Respond

    Respond with what fits

    A question, a boundary, a change in the environment, or a working piece of software.

  4. Stay

    Stay close enough to see

    Follow up, notice what changed, and let evidence shape the next move.
