One practice, several doors.

I spent fifteen years finding the real problem with people, then six years building and supporting the systems that were supposed to solve it. The thing I am good at sits in between: entering someone's actual situation, noticing what is live, and responding with whatever fits, whether that is a hand on a shoulder, a hard question, or a piece of software with an owner and a recovery path.

Oceanheart is the name for that way of working. It is not a technique yet. It becomes one through use, in public, with the people who come through these doors.

The same way of working at every door.

Whether the constraint is in a body, a relationship, or a workflow, the sequence does not change.

  1. Enter

    Enter the real context

    Not the description of it. The room, the inbox, the breath, the process as it actually runs.

  2. Find

    Find the live constraint

    Name the thing that is actually holding, its trigger, its cost, and who is carrying it.

  3. Respond

    Respond with what fits

    Touch, a conversation, a boundary, a behavioural experiment, an automation. The smallest response that could work.

  4. Stay

    Stay close enough to see

    Follow up. Notice what changed and what did not. Let the next step come from evidence, not the plan.
