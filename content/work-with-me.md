## Human systems

Bring me the mess. We'll make a system.

The best first project is rarely "build us an AI agent." It is a piece of work that happens repeatedly, crosses several tools or people, and has become too important to keep running from memory.

I work directly with the person who owns that process. Together we make the current workflow explicit, identify the expensive or fragile part, and decide whether the answer is a simpler process, lightweight automation, custom code, AI assistance, or some combination of them.

## What I build

### Workflow automation

Map one repeated process, connect the tools around it, remove avoidable handling, and keep human approval where judgement still matters. Typical work includes lead routing, onboarding, support triage, content operations, reporting, and internal handoffs.

### AI inside real work

Use models for classification, drafting, retrieval, or assistance without letting plausible output become business truth. Evaluation sets, deterministic checks, audit traces, monitoring, and escalation to a person are part of the system.

### Customer systems

Build the product around the workflow when an automation is not enough, including portals, payments, content delivery, and owner-facing tools.

This is a natural fit for wellbeing practitioners, educators, and other high-trust businesses whose founder has become the hidden connection between enquiries, forms, calendars, payments, content, and follow-up. I know that operating reality from inside a wellbeing practice. The useful distinction is between administration that should disappear and judgement that must stay with a person.

## Good first problems

- Leads, applications, or enquiries need to be qualified and routed.
- Onboarding depends on copied messages, forms, spreadsheets, and memory.
- Payments, access, content delivery, or follow-up do not agree with each other.
- A team is repeatedly summarising, classifying, checking, or moving information between systems.
- An existing automation fails silently or requires one person to keep repairing it.
- An AI prototype needs evaluation, business rules, monitoring, and a safe human fallback before people can rely on it.

## What I take responsibility for

I can own discovery, workflow design, implementation, integrations, deployment, monitoring, documentation, and handoff. I work across customer-facing products and internal operations, and I am comfortable explaining the same system to its owner and to the engineer who will maintain it.

Depending on the job, the implementation may include Python or TypeScript services, REST APIs, webhooks, PostgreSQL, LLM APIs, payments, authentication, cloud deployment, or a lightweight automation layer. I choose the smallest system that can do the job reliably.

## How an engagement starts

Send me three things:

1. Who currently does the work.
2. What repeats.
3. Where time, errors, waiting, or customer frustration accumulate.

I will start by understanding the process. If there is no worthwhile automation, I will say so. If there is, the first proposal will define one bounded workflow, its owner, the baseline, the safety boundary, and what evidence would count as an improvement.

For wellbeing work, I keep clinical judgement and sensitive client information outside the automation unless an approved professional, legal, and data-protection structure already exists. Administrative convenience does not outrank client trust.
