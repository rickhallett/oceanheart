## One-line read

Sell Richard as a calm, product-minded full-stack engineer who can turn messy data, external integrations, and ambiguous user workflows into reliable customer-facing software.

## Company snapshot

Volteras builds connected vehicle data infrastructure. The public pitch is that companies should not need aftermarket devices, screen scraping, or custom integrations for every vehicle manufacturer. Volteras provides a single API and product layer for consented vehicle data across more than 35 automakers.

The product surface is broader than raw telematics:

- API access to vehicle data;
- vehicle eligibility and user consent flows;
- fleet dashboards;
- charging and energy workflows;
- battery health reports and battery passport-style data;
- pricing based on active connected vehicles.

Commercially, the company sits at the intersection of EV fleets, leasing, insurance, finance, energy, charging, and battery valuation. Its Series A announcement says it raised USD 11.1M to expand connected vehicle data services for a cleaner and more efficient transport system.

## Role snapshot

The Mid-Level Full-Stack Engineer role is a practical product-engineering seat. Expect them to care about:

- TypeScript and modern frontend delivery;
- backend/API implementation;
- data-heavy product workflows;
- external integrations;
- reliability and maintainability;
- remote collaboration;
- enough product judgement to own slices without being spoon-fed.

This is not primarily an AI role. The AI/tooling piece is useful, but it should not dominate the screen unless Talent asks.

## Best fit

Richard's strongest fit is the combination of production full-stack work and recent systems work around unreliable inputs.

Strong overlaps:

- Full-stack shipping: React, Next, Vue, Angular, TypeScript, Node, Python, REST, PostgreSQL/SQLite.
- Product surfaces: client delivery, dashboards, data visualisation, portals, payments, CMS workflows, support and deployment.
- API and integration work: EDITED enterprise API integrations, Telesoft secure apps, client-facing Oceanheart work, and recent Node/TypeScript backends.
- Data and observability instincts: LoanSlam, The Pit, Sortie, and Halo all show traces, validators, failure tagging, CLI surfaces, and structured review loops.
- Remote/self-directed work: Oceanheart independent client work plus contract delivery.
- Human factors: therapy background gives a real, non-gimmicky story around ambiguous requirements, careful questioning, trust, and behaviour under uncertainty.

The best pattern match:

messy external system -> uncertain data/state -> product workflow -> validation/observability -> usable customer surface.

That is close to Volteras' world even without direct EV domain experience.

## Possible gaps

Be open about these:

- No direct EV fleet, telematics, OEM API, charging, or energy-market experience.
- No obvious previous automotive-domain product work.
- Mid-level role may want clean, consistent production feature delivery more than deep AI systems narrative.
- The CV foregrounds AI reliability heavily; Talent may need help seeing the ordinary full-stack product fit.
- If they use a specific backend stack or cloud stack not on the CV, that is a ramp rather than a claimed strength.

Do not over-defend the gaps. The answer is:

"I would not claim vehicle-data domain experience. My way into a domain is to model the workflow, find the failure modes, make the data visible, and ship narrow slices that domain experts can react to."

## Sell the journey

Keep it simple and grounded.

1. "I came from human systems."
   Fifteen years as a CBT therapist means Richard is used to ambiguous information, trust, behaviour change, risk, and careful questioning.

2. "Then I became a production engineer."
   Five years shipping TypeScript, Python, Node, React/Vue/Angular, APIs, secure apps, dashboards, integrations, and client-facing tools.

3. "Recently, I have been building reliable systems around uncertainty."
   LoanSlam, The Pit, Sortie, and Halo are not just AI demos. They are validation, tracing, cost awareness, review, and human/agent workflow tools.

4. "Volteras looks like a strong fit because the product problem is integration plus trust."
   Their customers need vehicle data that is accessible, reliable, consented, and useful in real workflows. That is a full-stack product problem with a lot of edge cases.

## 30-second pitch

I am a full-stack engineer with about five years shipping production TypeScript, Python, and Node systems, after an earlier career as a CBT therapist. My recent work has been around building reliable product workflows on top of uncertain inputs: APIs, validators, traces, dashboards, and AI-assisted tooling where output is reviewed rather than blindly trusted. I have not worked directly in EV fleet data, but Volteras' problem looks like the kind of work I enjoy: external integrations, messy real-world data, customer-facing product surfaces, and systems that need to be useful and trustworthy.

## If they ask "Why Volteras?"

Because Volteras is solving a real infrastructure problem rather than building another thin app layer. Connected vehicle data touches fleets, energy, insurance, finance, battery valuation, and charging. The hard part is making that data reliable and usable enough that customers can build business processes around it.

## If they ask "Why this role?"

It looks like a practical full-stack role with useful breadth: frontend, backend, APIs, integrations, and product judgement. That maps well to Richard's experience across SaaS, data visualisation, secure apps, client portals, and recent AI/product tooling.

## If they ask about AI

Use AI as a differentiator, not the headline.

Good framing:

"The AI work is relevant mostly because it trained the same reliability muscles: treat outputs as uncertain, make state visible, add validation, keep traces, and build tools that help humans make better decisions."

Avoid framing:

"I want an AI job."

This role is more product/integration/reliability than pure AI.

## If they ask about the EV/automotive gap

Say it cleanly:

"That is the gap. I have not worked in automotive or EV data. What I do bring is the ability to enter a domain through the workflow: what data comes in, what decisions rely on it, where it fails, what needs to be visible, and what checks make the system safer to use."

## Questions to ask Talent

- What parts of the platform would this role touch first: API, fleet portal, charging, battery health, or internal tooling?
- Is the immediate need more frontend product delivery, backend integration work, or full-stack ownership?
- What are the hardest reliability issues today: OEM API variation, vehicle eligibility, consent flows, stale data, customer onboarding, or operational visibility?
- How much automotive/EV domain knowledge is expected on entry?
- Do engineers work directly with customers or mostly through product?
- What does a strong first contribution look like for this role?
- How is the team using AI coding tools today, and what guardrails do they have around them?

## Phrases to use

- "external integrations with real-world edge cases"
- "make uncertain state visible"
- "turn messy workflows into dependable product surfaces"
- "data customers can trust enough to act on"
- "full-stack ownership across API, UI, and operational behaviour"
- "AI as a reliability/tooling accelerator, not magic"

## Phrases to avoid

- "I can pick up EVs quickly" without saying how.
- "My therapy background makes me good at product" as a standalone claim.
- "Fail-closed" more than once. It is useful, but this is not a regulated AI role.
- Over-indexing on Kubernetes/NATS unless asked. The role sounds more product/API-focused.
- Pitching yourself as senior if the role is explicitly mid-level.

## Best closing

I am interested because this looks like real full-stack product engineering around data people need to trust. I would bring production TypeScript/Python/Node experience, recent reliability and AI-tooling work, and a practical way of entering unfamiliar domains through workflows, failure modes, and customer-visible value.
